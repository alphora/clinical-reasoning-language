# Policy document structure

General medical-policy interpretation guidance. This file contains no policy ruling or answer key.

Read punctuation, conjunctions, lead-in prose, bullets, numbering and indentation together. Inspect the formatted source when flattened text obscures nesting; indentation alone does not override explicit prose.

- Preserve shared lead-in qualifications on every alternative they govern.
- An AND within one bullet joins that bullet's requirements. Do not extend it across sibling alternatives.
- Nested children belong to their parent. Preserve the parent's local conjunction and the children's stated AND/OR.
- A sentence-ending "and" can enumerate named alternatives or join required conditions; derive its role from the governing sentence.
- Keep the reconstructed expression and source spans in the existing source map. If wording and formatting genuinely conflict, retain the ambiguity for scoped source adjudication.

Synthetic example: "Either A; or B and at least one of C or D" means A OR (B AND (C OR D)). With a shared requirement S, it means S AND (A OR (B AND (C OR D))). These letters have no clinical meaning and are not a prescribed shape for real policies.

Review the source map and actual conditions against the formatted source. Existing independent cases should distinguish complete alternatives, local requirements and shared qualifications; do not create a separate audit framework.
