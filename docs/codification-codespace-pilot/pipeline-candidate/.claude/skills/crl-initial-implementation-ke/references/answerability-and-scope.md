# Answerability and scope

Customer-neutral source-analysis guidance. Examples below are synthetic. The bound customer profile, accepted method, authorized source and matched kit control; this file contains no individual policy ruling.

## The answerability test

For every candidate concept: does the provider need to answer it, and is it one answer or two? Do not substitute assumptions about what a particular chart system stores for an answerable provider question.

**A. No separate answer.** Descriptive language, context already established on the route, duplicate facts, and illustrative descriptions of clinical method do not automatically become attestations. Distinguish a policy-required method from examples of how a clinician reaches a conclusion. Preserve genuinely required methods; do not add a question for every mentioned example.

**B. One answer.** Preserve the complete source statement and its load-bearing qualifiers.

- **B-0: Requested indication versus patient diagnosis.** What a request is for differs from a diagnosis the patient happens to have. Represent the source's requested-use scope with answerable request-side evidence. Do not require a submitter to classify their request as a regulatory or coverage conclusion. Missing evidence is not an asserted off-indication request.
- **B-1: Lead-sentence scope.** A lead sentence's population, site, indication or timing applies to its governed bullets. Carry it in the scoped fact or a shared gate whose coverage is demonstrated. Avoid an unlinked duplicate of the same fact. Ask whether the bullet could otherwise be satisfied outside the lead sentence; if yes, its qualifier remains consequential. Do not silently drop it.
- **B-2: Identification versus illustration.** "i.e." identifies what the criterion means; "such as" introduces examples. Synthetic contrast: "both required assessments, i.e. A and B" requires both; "adequate assessment, using methods such as A or B" does not by itself require both methods. Inspect the actual governing prose.

**C. Different kinds of answer.** Separate a data lookup from a distinct clinical judgment when the source requires both. Synthetic example: receipt of intervention A and a clinician's assessment of its effectiveness are different evidence needs.

For enumerated categories, follow source wording and applicable bound customer rulings; do not import another customer's Other-option convention. For punctuation, local AND and nesting use policy-document-structure.md.
