Fit for Use in a Compiler

Clarity and Lack of Left Recursion: The grammar is relatively clear and straightforward in its structure. Potential Ambiguities and Conflicts: Apart from the structural issues with when clauses discussed earlier, the grammar could face a couple of practical problems in a compiler setting. One concern is the handling of indentation tokens in the lexer versus the parser (discussed more in the next section). Specifically, the WS rule (which skips spaces and tabs) might conflict with the INDENT token rule. For example, at the start of an indented line, the input has spaces that could match both WS and INDENT. ANTLR will try to match the longest possible token; if it treats all leading spaces as one WS token (since WS matches one-or-more whitespace of any length) instead of as an INDENT, the parser would never receive the INDENT token it expects, causing a parse failure. These shortcomings mean that additional logic is required beyond the raw grammar to avoid parser errors. They don’t make the grammar inherently ambiguous in the sense of multiple parse trees, but they do represent points where a compiler implementer must be careful to get the intended behavior.
Integration and Maintainability: Assuming the indentation handling is fixed, the rest of the grammar appears quite suitable for integration into a compiler. Fit for Python-like Whitespace Control

In practice, a real Python-style parser requires logic to count the spaces at the start of a line and compare it to the previous indent level.
